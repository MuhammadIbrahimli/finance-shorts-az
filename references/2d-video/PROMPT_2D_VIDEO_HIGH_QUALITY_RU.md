# Reusable Prompt for High-Quality 2D Video

Use the attached narration as the sole source of content and timing. Create a complete 16:9, 1080p YouTube video. If materials for this same narration (script, transcript, references) are attached to the project, use them only as supporting materials; do not use audio or text from other chats, Telegram, or unrelated files. Preserve the original narration unchanged: do not trim, overwrite, speed up, or replace the voice. On-screen labels and subtitles, if needed, must be in the narration's language and contain only relevant text.

## Understand the narration first

1. Listen to the entire file; identify language, duration, pauses, and structure. Produce an accurate transcript and align words and phrases to actual audio timecodes. Do not split the runtime evenly among phrases or guess timings from sentence count.
2. Before building scenes, prepare the complete table: timecode | spoken phrase | concrete frame content | shot/angle | movement. Cover the entire narration, including opening, pauses, and ending. Preserve the original wording verbatim.
3. Give every phrase its own visual treatment: who/what appears, where it happens, what action is visible, and which object carries the meaning. Show literal details literally; express abstract thoughts with a clear metaphor for that specific idea.

## Visual language and motion

Create new scenes for the content of every phrase. Choose an appropriate environment, lighting, architecture, and props for each location. Do not use a universal background or repeat scene templates. Do not show buses, phones, maps, cafes, schools, radios, or other objects outside the phrases where they are mentioned or needed for continuous action. Repeat a place or character only when story continuity requires it.

Make the video cinematic 2D/2.5D with convincing depth and separate 3D/low-poly shots where justified by the scale of the scene (streets, buildings, transport, underground passages, complex spaces). Vary shot size and angle deliberately: wide for location, medium for action, close-up for key detail. Use parallax and camera motion for depth, but do not let camera motion replace scene action.

Animate what the narration describes: characters walk, turn, pick up and set down objects; vehicles move; doors and water move and interact with the environment. Give motion weight: preparation, contact with surfaces/objects, inertia, and a natural finish. Keep geometry, scale, perspective, direction of motion, hand/foot placement, ground contact, and object continuity stable. Avoid floating objects, stray protrusions, model intersections, broken limbs, scale jumps, flickering outlines, and unexplained movement. Keep silhouettes clean, edges precise, composition readable, and lighting consistent. Do not conceal flaws with filters, darkness, or fast cuts.

Do not fill frames with random decoration. Show graphics, maps, numbers, and labels only when the corresponding fact is spoken; keep text short, accurate, and readable. Do not add facts, symbols, or signs absent from the narration or unhelpful to understanding. Keep the overall style and character designs consistent, while designing each location and composition specifically for its phrase.

## Visual quality bar

Aim for a beautiful, realistic, professionally assembled result within expressive 2D/2.5D animation. Every frame should look like a finished cinematic illustration: a clear focal point, balanced composition, deliberate depth, clean forms and edges, expressive lighting and shadows, coherent palette, atmospheric perspective, and fitting material textures. Match light, color, and contrast to each scene's emotion; maintain continuity within a shot and change them when the setting changes.

Make scale, perspective, weight, inertia, collisions, support, and interaction with the environment believable. For dimensional shots, use convincing perspective, foreground/midground/background separation, soft parallax, directional light, and natural depth of field only where useful. Avoid flat cut-out silhouettes, cardboard-looking sets, primitive geometric stand-ins where recognizable objects are needed, overly empty or cluttered frames, random highlights, dirty outlines, plastic materials, and a cheap template look. Stylization is welcome, but every object should be coherent, fit the perspective, and look visually convincing.

Before delivery, inspect every frame as an art director: does it look beautiful and complete when paused; is the main object immediately clear; are there visual artifacts, awkward edge contacts, random protrusions, inconsistent light, or messy intersections? If a frame does not look ready to publish, revise the composition, model, lighting, or animation and review it again.

## Quality control before the full render

Work in batches of scenes if the studio requires it, and make a contact sheet after each batch. Open and inspect every sheet: compare each frame with its phrase and timecode; flag shots that are irrelevant, needlessly repeated, static, visually dirty, or geometrically broken. Fix them and inspect the sheet again. Also check key intermediate moments within every long shot so a defect is not hidden by one favorable still.

Before the full render, prepare and show:
- the complete “timecode — phrase — frame” table;
- a contact sheet of all scenes;
- short previews from different parts of the video: beginning, middle, key story scene, and ending, with the original narration and sound design.

Watch the previews in motion and with sound. Confirm that each segment contains audible original narration; if music, effects, or ambience were added, verify they are present, appropriate, and quieter than the voice. Fix defects before the final render. Do not start the full render until timing, frame meaning, motion, physics, geometry, and audio have passed this review. After fixes, recheck affected scenes and previews.

After approval of the storyboard and previews, render the full video. Then check the final file: duration matches narration; audio is present throughout; images do not freeze or repeat by accident; and key sections have no stray/intersecting parts, broken perspective, flicker, or other visible artifacts. If review finds a defect, fix it and repeat the final render and review. At the end, provide the path to the finished video and previews.

If unspecified, use 16:9, 1080p, and the language of the attached narration. Do not stop at increasing the number of still images: accurate word timing, lively action, believable object interaction, and clean visuals matter.
